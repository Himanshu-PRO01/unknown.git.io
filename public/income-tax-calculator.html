Common questions

Is income up to 12 lakh really tax-free?

Under the new regime, the Section 87A rebate makes the tax zero when taxable income is up to 12 lakh. Salaried people also get a 75,000 standard deduction, so salary up to 12.75 lakh can come to zero tax. A marginal relief applies just above the limit.

Which regime should I choose?

The new regime has lower rates and very few deductions. The old regime has higher rates but allows deductions such as 80C and home loan interest. Try both here with your deductions to compare.

What is the standard deduction?

Salaried people and pensioners get 75,000 under the new regime and 50,000 under the old regime.

Does this include surcharge?

No. Surcharge applies at higher incomes, so this tool is best for incomes up to 50 lakh. Rules can change, so check the latest ones before you rely on the result.

How this calculator works

It subtracts the standard deduction (and, in the old regime, the deductions you enter) from your income, applies the slab rates, then the Section 87A rebate, and finally adds 4% health and education cess. You can see the tax in each slab in the table.

Slabs used

New regime: nil up to 4 lakh, then 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% and 30% in steps of 4 lakh. Old regime: nil up to 2.5 lakh, 5% up to 5 lakh, 20% up to 10 lakh, and 30% above that.

This is a general estimate, not tax advice. Tax rules and your own situation can change the result.
