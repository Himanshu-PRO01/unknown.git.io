Common questions

What is PPF?

The Public Provident Fund is a government-backed long-term savings scheme with a 15-year lock-in. You can extend it in blocks of 5 years.

How much can I invest in PPF?

You can invest between 500 and 1,50,000 rupees in a financial year. The calculator uses 1,50,000 if you enter more.

What is the PPF interest rate?

The government reviews it every quarter. It stays at 7.1% for October to December 2026, but it can change, so check the latest rate before you decide.

How is PPF interest calculated?

Interest is worked out each month on the lowest balance between the 5th and the end of the month, and it is credited once a year. This calculator assumes you deposit the whole year's amount at the start of the year, which gives full interest for that year.

Is PPF interest taxable?

Under current rules the interest and the maturity amount are tax-free, and deposits can qualify for a deduction under Section 80C in the old tax regime. Rules can change, so check with a chartered accountant.

How the PPF calculator works

Each year you deposit a fixed amount, and the balance earns interest that is added once a year. The next year, interest is earned on the larger balance, which is compounding. The longer you stay invested, the more the interest adds up.

The formula

Maturity value = P x [((1 + r)^n - 1) / r] x (1 + r), where P is the yearly deposit, r is the yearly rate as a decimal and n is the number of years.
