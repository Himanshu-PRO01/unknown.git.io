GST invoice format: what to include, with an example

The details a GST invoice normally needs, and how CGST, SGST and IGST work.

Last updated: October 2026

A GST invoice, also called a tax invoice, is the bill a registered business gives for goods or services. It lets the buyer claim input tax credit, so missing details can cause problems for both sides. This guide covers what it normally includes. Rules depend on your type of business, so confirm them with a chartered accountant.

What to include

  • Your name, address and GSTIN.
  • A unique invoice number and the invoice date.
  • The buyer's name and address, and their GSTIN if they are registered.
  • A description of each item or service, with its HSN or SAC code, quantity and unit.
  • The value of each item, any discount, and the taxable value.
  • The GST rate and the amount of CGST and SGST, or IGST.
  • The place of supply, and the total amount.
  • Your signature or digital signature where required.

CGST and SGST, or IGST?

If you and the buyer are in the same state, GST is split into equal CGST and SGST. If you are in different states, you charge IGST for the whole amount.

A simple example

You charge 10,000 rupees for a service at 18% GST, to a client in your own state. CGST is 900 and SGST is 900, so the invoice total is 11,800. If the client is in another state, you show IGST of 1,800 and the same total of 11,800.

Timing and numbering

Invoices are generally raised within a set number of days after supplying services, usually 30, and invoice numbers should follow a consistent series. Check the current rules for your business, including any e-invoicing requirement that depends on turnover.
