How is EMI calculated? The formula with an example

The EMI formula, a worked example and why a longer loan costs more.

Last updated: October 2026

An EMI (equated monthly instalment) is the fixed amount you pay your lender every month until a loan is cleared. It looks simple, but it hides two things: how much of each payment is interest, and how much extra you pay for a longer tenure.

The formula

EMI = P x r x (1 + r)^n / ((1 + r)^n - 1)

  • P is the loan amount.
  • r is the monthly interest rate: the yearly rate divided by 12 and by 100.
  • n is the number of monthly instalments.

A worked example

Take a loan of 10,00,000 rupees at 9% a year for 10 years. Here r = 9 / 12 / 100 = 0.0075 and n = 120. The formula gives an EMI of about ₹12,668 a month. Over 120 months you pay ₹15,20,109 in total, so the interest cost is ₹5,20,109.

Why early EMIs are mostly interest

Interest is charged each month on the balance you still owe. In month one that is 10,00,000 x 0.0075 = 7,500 rupees. Of your first EMI, 7,500 is interest and the remaining ₹5,168 reduces the loan. Each month the balance falls, so the interest part shrinks and the repayment part grows.

What a longer tenure does

Stretch the same loan to 15 years and the EMI falls to about ₹10,143. But you now pay ₹8,25,680 in interest, which is ₹3,05,571 more than on the 10-year loan. A lower EMI feels easier, but it costs more in total.

Flat rate or reducing rate?

A reducing rate charges interest only on the balance you still owe, and it is what the formula above uses. A flat rate charges interest on the full original amount for the whole tenure, so it costs much more than the same number quoted as a reducing rate. If a lender quotes a flat rate, ask for the equivalent reducing rate before you compare offers.
