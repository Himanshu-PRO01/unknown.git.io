How to write a freelance invoice: a checklist

What to include, how to set payment terms and how to follow up.

Last updated: October 2026

Getting paid on time starts with a clear invoice. Here is a checklist you can follow for every client.

What every invoice needs

  • The word "Invoice" and a unique invoice number.
  • The invoice date and the payment due date.
  • Your name or business name, address and contact details.
  • Your client's name and address.
  • A clear description of the work, with the quantity or hours and the rate.
  • The subtotal, any tax and the total amount due.
  • How to pay: bank details, UPI ID or a payment link.

Set clear payment terms

Say when payment is due, for example within 7, 15 or 30 days. Agree the terms in writing before the work starts, and repeat them on the invoice. Short terms such as 7 days are common for small projects, and many freelancers ask for an advance on larger ones.

Add taxes where they apply

If you are registered for GST and the client is in India, add the GST and your GSTIN. For international clients, ask your chartered accountant how to show tax on services you export.

Use the right currency

Bill in the currency you agreed, and note any bank or platform fees that the client should cover. Make the total easy to find.

Send it quickly and follow up

Send the invoice as soon as the work is accepted. If payment is late, send a polite reminder on the due date, and a firmer one a week later. You can also state a late fee in your terms.

Keep records

Keep a copy of every invoice and payment. You will need them for tax filing and if a client disputes a payment.
